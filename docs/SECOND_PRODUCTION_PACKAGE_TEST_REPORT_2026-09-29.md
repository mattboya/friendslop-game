# Second production package: independent Unity review

Date: September 29, 2026. Verdict: **all six FBXs import, assemble, and run in an isolated macOS development sample. Gameplay and collision acceptance remain open.** The first package, existing Resources assets, main scene, and build list were not changed. Shared simulation, HUD, poi, smoke, and test edits continued independently during this review.

## Confirmed in Unity and the native sample

- Unity 6000.3.24f1 compiled `Assets/Festival/Art/ProductionSample02/SecondProductionSample.unity` and built `Builds/macOS/ProductionSample02/SecondProductionSample.app`. Native URP dusk captures at 1280 × 720 show the [wide stage](second-production-package-review/overview-emission.png), [front approach](second-production-package-review/approach-emission.png), [side and underside](second-production-package-review/side-emission.png), [rear](second-production-package-review/rear-emission.png), and [DJ controls](second-production-package-review/dj-controls-emission.png). No missing material or obvious reversed-face patch is visible in these views; this is visual coverage, not a full normals inspection.
- Imported renderer/triangle counts match the manifest for each asset: stage 10/40,512; speaker stack 4/7,674 each; DJ station 58/23,080; flight case 5/4,256; Sun 6/3,352; Moon 6/3,660. The seven-asset composition plus a Unity ground plane has **94 renderers and 90,408 triangles**, with one directional light. All inspected materials remap to URP Lit. See [import audit](second-production-package-review/import-audit.txt).
- The source Blender `AH02_Production` scene opens and retains 1,266 mesh objects for editing. Export meshes are joined by material except the DJ controls. In the imported hierarchy, platter, fader, pad, and EQ pivots have their geometry as children. A representative 30° rotation or 5 cm translation moved a mesh vertex by 3.5–5.8 cm, then the review fixture restored the transform.
- At the handoff placements, the stage fronts the approach camera, the roof and truss read as supported, and the flight case and console rest on the deck in captured views. The sign reads from the approach. Speaker baffles and recessed drivers are visible at the side. The Sun ray silhouette and Moon crescent are distinct [with emission](second-production-package-review/overview-emission.png) and [without emission](second-production-package-review/overview-no-emission.png); close views: [Sun on](second-production-package-review/sun-emission.png), [Sun off](second-production-package-review/sun-no-emission.png), [Moon on](second-production-package-review/moon-emission.png), [Moon off](second-production-package-review/moon-no-emission.png).
- The 18 × 9 m stage has a 1.4 m deck. Generator dimensions specify seven 0.2 m rises, 0.34 m runs, and a 1.6 m stair width. This is plausible visual scale, but has not been traversed. The sample contains no stage/stair collider proxies.
- A short isolated 180-frame native run averaged **8.43 ms frame interval**, max **25.02 ms**, with **74.8 MiB** allocated Unity memory. See [metrics](second-production-package-review/runtime-metrics.txt). It is a sparse, stationary scene with no avatars, crowd, UI, network, or effects; it cannot establish populated-festival performance.
- Unity EditMode **31/31** and PlayMode **19/19** suites passed against the shared workspace after this fixture was added. These regressions confirm compilation and existing tests; they do not exercise the new meshes in a mission.

## Findings for the authoring and integration passes

1. **DJ reach needs validation.** The imported performer-feet marker is at world `(0, 1.40, -1.19)`, left hand contact at `(1.18, 2.29, -0.15)`, and right contact at `(-0.10, 2.30, 0.04)`. Horizontal feet-to-target distances are about **1.57 m left** and **1.23 m right**. A normal standing avatar may need a different performer position, torso lean, or contact target. No avatar or IK was present, so an actual reach failure is unconfirmed.
2. **The clue plaques are not readable from the wide approach.** The silhouettes remain distinct without emission, which is the useful shared-world cue. The small `SUN` and `MOON` labels are clear only close up. The halo and stage lights dominate the overview when emission is on; check the landmarks against populated festival lighting before adjusting intensity.
3. **Unity imported X is mirrored against the Blender manifest.** For example, `AH02_PlatterLeft` imports at local X `+1.18` although the Blender manifest lists `-1.18`. In the front native view the named left deck still appears on the viewer's left. Use imported transforms and an explicit stage-facing map for gameplay hookup; do not copy Blender coordinates directly into runtime contact code.
4. **The stage is visual geometry only.** No stage, staircase, handrail, speaker, or totem collision proxy was created. Roof/truss contacts and caster/deck placement look plausible in the captured views, but no physical walkability, performer safety, controller interaction, or camera occlusion acceptance follows from that.
5. **Front-facing controls have no action yet.** Named pivots move their child geometry when directly transformed, and clue/contact empties import at the expected stage/totem locations. No platter spin, fader animation, button press, DJ takeover, private clue display, purchase, or hand contact is connected to gameplay.

## Remaining gates and next pass

Use a reversible development-only stage replacement fixture in the actual festival, with explicit rotation and anchor mapping. Add collision proxies and test the stairs and deck with a player; seat a current avatar at the performer marker and verify both hand contacts, camera sightline and DJ takeover; then inspect Sun/Moon clue presentation separately for each client to keep private clue state player-local. Measure a populated scene with effects and two clients before considering a global art replacement. Human visual acceptance against the supplied references remains a separate gate.
