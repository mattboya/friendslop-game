# Third production package: independent Unity review

Date: September 29, 2026. Verdict: **the 43 FBXs import and render in an isolated macOS development sample; nine cast faces and grips retain their requested blendshape weights while the shared rig animates.** The world package is visual geometry. Character, wardrobe, collision, interaction, and performance acceptance in the actual festival remain open.

## Confirmed in the isolated sample

- Unity 6000.3.24f1 imported **32 world FBXs, nine assembled cast FBXs, one rig-only motion FBX, and one wardrobe library FBX**. All renderer materials resolved to URP Lit, and no mesh with vertices imported with zero normals. The [import audit](third-production-package-review/import-audit.txt) records per-renderer materials, normals, bones, shape channels, and clip bindings. The sample scene built as `Builds/macOS/ProductionSample03/ThirdProductionSample.app` and completed a native 1280 × 720 capture run without an application exception.
- Each cast has **7–9 skinned renderers**, **33,484–39,320 triangles**, and **nine blendshape channels**: seven shared face controls plus `GripL` and `GripR`. The [people gallery](third-production-package-review/people-gallery.png) and [conversation face sheets](third-production-package-review/face-AttendeeAverage-conversation-sheet.png) show the assets under a Unity dusk/URP setup. The face controls produce visible eyelid and mouth changes at conversation distance; [gameplay-distance sheets](third-production-package-review/face-AttendeeAverage-gameplay-sheet.png) show that individual expressions are hard to distinguish at this camera distance.
- The six 2-second motion clips (`Assist`, `Cheer`, `Dance`, `Idle`, `Offer`, `Panic`) each imported with **160 transform curves and zero blendshape curves**. A shared controller on each cast's `FestivalRig` played Idle and Dance; all nine had a **10.62° sampled spine change**. For each cast, Neutral plus all seven face shapes read back at the requested value after Animator evaluation, and both grip shapes stayed at 100 during Dance. See [native observations](third-production-package-review/native-observations.txt). This specifically avoids the first package's zero-value face/grip curve conflict in this fixture. It does not prove every animation transition or live interaction.
- The wardrobe library imported **91 skinned renderers** and **546 Fit shape channels**. Six sampled body-fit combinations and all **80 styles across eight categories** were switched in a development-only selector and captured. The [fit sheet](third-production-package-review/wardrobe-fit-combinations.png) and [category sheets](third-production-package-review/wardrobe-Headgear-sheet.png) show the imported pieces rendered on the existing master rig. No large missing piece or broken skinning is evident in these front views. One body shape and one cumulative outfit state were used for the style sweep, so the 80 × six-fit compatibility matrix remains unverified.
- Representative world assets rendered from front and rear, including the [medical tent front](third-production-package-review/world-Medical-front.png) and [rear](third-production-package-review/world-Medical-rear-side.png), [shuttle front](third-production-package-review/world-Shuttle-front.png) and [rear](third-production-package-review/world-Shuttle-rear-side.png), and [stash front](third-production-package-review/world-Stash-front.png) and [rear](third-production-package-review/world-Stash-rear-side.png). The clinic interior and open shuttle doorway are visible. The imported shuttle door pivots each have two child renderers and moved their bounds centers **0.104 m** on a 30° local rotation; the stash lid moved **0.298 m**. The wristband socket marker imported. See the [pivot audit](third-production-package-review/pivot-audit.txt).
- A stationary 180-frame native gallery sample averaged **8.47 ms/frame** (max 33.35 ms); a world overview averaged **9.03 ms/frame** (max 91.62 ms), with 326 active renderers and 218.8 MiB Unity allocated memory at capture. These are fixture measurements, with no network session or gameplay simulation, and are unsuitable as a festival performance target.
- The shared Unity regressions passed after the fixture changes: **31/31 EditMode** and **19/19 PlayMode**. These suites establish compilation and existing behavior, but do not exercise the new package inside a mission.

## Findings and integration work

1. **Gameplay-distance expressions need a readability pass.** The close captures clearly show Blink, Delight, and other mouth/eye changes. At the fixture's wider gameplay camera, the shapes are too small to read individually. The darkest skin tones lose some facial detail under this dusk light. Test actual dialogue and clue camera framing before changing facial geometry or lighting.
2. **The wardrobe sweep does not establish universal fit.** Front captures support that 80 slots load and render; they do not cover side/back clipping, all six body fits per style, animation extremes, hand props, or every accessory combination. The existing runtime wardrobe still selects only its original four slots. Connect the expanded categories deliberately, then validate a fit/pose matrix in the game.
3. **The controller belongs on the nested rig.** The imported cast FBX root has no Animator, while the motion clip paths begin at `Hips/...`. The working fixture attaches the shared controller to each cast's `FestivalRig` child. An integration that attaches it to the outer prefab root may silently fail to animate the expected bones; include that binding in the gameplay hookup check.
4. **World interaction remains to be built.** The imported shuttle doors and stash lid rotate with child geometry, but no interaction, collider, navigation, boarding, stash opening, or recovery-band attachment behavior is wired into the playable festival. The open doorway is a rest pose, not proof that a player can enter. Inspect clinic access, tent interiors, small gear at first-person range, support contacts, and forest sightlines in the actual level.
5. **Two triangle counts differ from the source manifest.** Unity reports `GroveDetail` at **1,680** versus **1,722** source triangles and `Shuttle` at **10,768** versus **10,776**. The assets import and render; this small mismatch may come from export/import triangulation or source manifest timing. Reconcile the manifest before using counts as an automated content gate. Other inspected totals match.
6. **Blender coordinates cannot be copied directly into Unity gameplay code.** The named shuttle door pivots import at Unity local positions `(±1.310, 0.400, 1.610)` with the X sign reversed from the Blender manifest; the stash pivot imports at `(0, 1.000, -0.575)`. Use the imported hierarchy and visible facing to establish door and lid action axes.

## Validation boundary and fixture

`Production03Audit.cs` creates palette-matched URP materials, imports the FBXs, records mesh/animation facts, and checks pivots. `Production03Builder.cs` creates an isolated sample scene and development macOS build. `Production03Capture.cs` and `Production03WardrobeSelector.cs` are compiled only in Editor/development builds; capture runs only with `--sample-output`. No authored Blender file, generator, or FBX was changed during testing. The main playable scene and runtime `Resources` assets were not replaced. Raw captures and native logs remain under `artifacts/third-production-sample/`; selected evidence is in `docs/third-production-package-review/`.

The next acceptance step is a reversible festival-level integration with player-scale collision, camera and interaction checks, all six fits in representative animated poses, and a two-client native smoke/human pass. Treat private clue visibility and readable faces as explicit checks in that pass.
